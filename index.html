Tharaka D. Fonseka

PhD Student, Princeton ECE

I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Princeton University, where I am a member of the JHA Lab led by Prof. Niraj K. Jha.

Before Princeton, I earned my BSc in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the University of Peradeniya, Sri Lanka, graduating with First Class Honors, a perfect GPA of 4.0/4.0, and ranked first in my batch. I was advised by Prof. Roshan Godaliyadda, Prof. Parakrama Ekanayake & Prof. Vijitha Herath. I then worked as a Research Assistant at the Multidisciplinary AI Research Centre (MARC), University of Peradeniya.

My research interests lie at the intersection of machine learning, knowledge graphs, LLM reasoning, and AI for healthcare. I am currently focused on developing reliable knowledge-grounded reasoning systems for LLMs, specifically investigating:

  • How context-augmented supervision can help models learn richer and more reliable representations
  • Methods for augmenting knowledge graphs with contextual evidence to improve multi-hop reasoning
  • Reinforcement learning strategies for enhancing compositional reasoning over structured knowledge paths

I am always happy to chat about research ideas or about cricket 🏏 — feel free to reach out!

Research

PoPStat: Linking Population Pyramids to Disease Mortality

Devised PoPDivergence and PoPStat, two metrics that condense a country's full population pyramid into scalar values and quantify their association with cause-specific mortality across 371 diseases and 200+ countries — outperforming traditional indicators such as median age, GDP per capita, and HDI. The framework was later extended to quantify demographic vulnerability to COVID-19.

Devising PoPStat (arXiv) · PoPStat–COVID19 (arXiv)

Benchmarking Psychomotor Skills with Wearable Devices

A framework for optimizing psychomotor learning through human motion analysis. IMU motion trajectories are mapped to a performance space via task-specific quality metrics, enabling the identification of benchmark clusters and targeted remedial actions — validated on a table tennis forehand stroke case study.

IEEE Access 2025 · arXiv

Social Interactions & Mental Well-being During COVID-19

Analyzed a survey of 3,020 households across Sri Lanka using factor analysis and unsupervised clustering, identifying distinct psychological groups and how interpersonal relationships mitigated psychological distress — with actionable insights for mental-health policy in future crises.

Frontiers in Psychology 2024

Welding Quality Assessment from Biomechanical Data

Used wearable IMU sensors to capture hand movements during welding tasks and trained ML/DL models to predict welding quality parameters, proposing a novel method for grading welders from beginner to expert. Received the Best Paper Award at MERCon 2024.

MERCon 2024

Human Joint Angle Estimation with EKF and PCA

Combined an Extended Kalman Filter with Principal Component Analysis to estimate human joint angles from a wireless wearable IMU device, validated against YOLOv7-based vision estimates.

Hand Gesture Recognition & Gait Analysis (Undergraduate Thesis)

Undergraduate thesis on hand gesture recognition and gait analysis via wearable devices, advised by Prof. Roshan Godaliyadda, Prof. Parakrama Ekanayake, and Prof. Vijitha Herath. Ranked among the top 5 undergraduate electrical engineering projects island-wide and won the Gold Award at Techno Sri Lanka 2023.
